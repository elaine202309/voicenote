How to Take Podcast Notes With a Voice Note

Audio cannot be skimmed. A podcast note without a timestamp is a note you will never check, and three hours of listening can vanish into one vague impression — unless you capture the moments while they are playing.

November 23, 2026 7 min read Elaine
Download on the App Store

Key takeaways

  • You cannot skim audio, so the note has to be made while it plays, not afterwards.
  • Pause the episode when something lands; a thirty-second recording beats a good intention.
  • Say the timestamp out loud — it is the only way to find the moment again.
  • An episode earns notes only if it ends in one thing you will do differently.

Listening feels like learning and behaves like forgetting. You finish a ninety-minute episode convinced you picked up something important, and by Thursday all you can say is “it was about growth”.

The fix is not a better podcast app; it is interrupting the audio. A note made during the episode — one pause, one sentence, one timestamp — survives. A note made afterwards is a summary of your mood.

How to do it in VoiceNote

Step 1 — Pause when something lands

Listen with VoiceNote one tap away. When a sentence changes how you think about something, pause the episode and record for thirty seconds to a minute. The free plan allows up to two minutes per note, so a pause fits comfortably; Prime extends a single recording to fifteen if you want to recap a whole episode at the end.

  • Say the timestamp first. “At twenty-three minutes, the guest said…” — the exact time is what lets you go back, quote it or send it to someone.
  • Say why it landed. Not just the claim: “this contradicts what we assumed about pricing” is the note; the quote alone is a bookmark.
  • Keep it short enough to keep doing it. If a pause takes four minutes, you will stop pausing by episode three.
  • Capture your disagreement too. “She says onboarding is the problem, but our data says retention” is worth more than the part you agreed with.

Step 2 — Apply Content Summary to the pauses

At the end of the episode, open the notes and tap AI Style Conversion, then choose Content Summary. The style turns your spoken pauses into concise key points, in the order you recorded them.

If you recorded several pauses as separate notes, summarise them one at a time and keep the timestamps in the text. The style compresses what you said; it does not know which episode you were listening to, so the header line — show, episode, date — stays your job.

Step 3 — Finish with a timestamp list and one action

Assemble the notes into something you would actually reopen:

  • Header: show, episode title, date you listened.
  • Three to five bullets, each with its timestamp, in your words rather than the guest's.
  • One quote you would send to a colleague, copied accurately if you plan to use it.
  • One action: the single thing you will do differently, with a date. This line is the difference between a listened-to podcast and a used one.

Why timestamps are not optional

Text can be skimmed and searched. Audio cannot: to find the sentence you half-remember, you have to scrub through the episode again, guessing at the shape of the conversation. Without a time, a note is a claim you cannot check.

Timestamps also make notes shareable. “Listen from twenty-three minutes” is a useful message; “there's a bit about pricing somewhere in the middle” is not.

And they protect you from two common failures of podcast notes: the vague summary written from memory at the end, and the wall of auto-transcript that nobody reads twice. A pause plus a time sits between the two, and it is short enough to do while listening.

A worked example

A made-up example. A marketer listens to a seventy-minute episode about pricing while walking, and pauses three times.

Spoken pauses

“Eleven minutes: he says most companies discount because a competitor did, not because the maths changed. That is us, in June. Twenty-three minutes: the bit about the price increase being easier to sell than the discount being removed — customers read a price rise as confidence and a removed discount as a trick. Thirty-eight minutes: I disagreed here. He says packaging is the lever, but our support load went up with the last package change, so packaging is not free. The thing I want to try: sending the annual option before the discount conversation, not after.”

Content Summary, assembled

The Pricing Show · ep. 112, “Discounts are a habit” · listened Nov 23

· 11:00 — Companies discount because a competitor did, not because the maths changed. That was our June decision.
· 23:00 — A price rise reads as confidence; a removed discount reads as a trick.
· 38:00 — He treats packaging as the free lever. Our support load rose after the last package change, so it is not free.

One action: offer the annual plan before the discount conversation in the next renewal call.
Quote to send: “a removed discount reads as a trick”.

What changed: three moments that would have evaporated became three checkable claims, and the episode ended in one dated action.

Before you close the app

  • Check your timestamps. If you estimated, scrub back once and correct them while the episode is still open.
  • Mark what is theirs and what is yours. “He says” versus “I think” — the distinction is the whole point of having thoughts while listening.
  • Skip the episodes that do not earn it. Some podcasts are company, not input. Deciding that deliberately is a skill, not laziness.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Taking notes after the episode. You will write the summary of an impression.
  • Writing no timestamps. The note becomes unverifiable the moment you press stop.
  • Transcribing instead of noting. A full transcript is not a note; it is a place to lose the point again.
  • Capturing everything. Three or four pauses per hour is a working rhythm; twenty is a data entry job.
  • Ending without an action. If nothing changes, the episode was entertainment, which is fine — as long as you noticed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I note while listening or afterwards?

While listening, in pauses. Notes written afterwards lose the timestamps, and the timestamps are what make the notes useful.

How long should each pause be?

Thirty seconds to a minute. The free plan allows up to two minutes per note, so a pause never hits the limit; only a full-episode recap needs Prime's fifteen.

How is this different from summarising an article?

An article can be skimmed and searched, so a summary after reading works. Audio cannot, so the capture has to happen during the episode and carry timestamps. Same style, different job.

Can VoiceNote record the podcast itself?

It records your voice, not the audio you are playing. Speak your note in a pause with the episode stopped.

What about the podcast app's own transcript?

A transcript is a record of what was said, not a record of what mattered to you. It is useful for checking a quote you already noted — not as a substitute for the note.

Which languages does VoiceNote support?

VoiceNote transcribes 46 languages. Note in the language you think in, even if you listen in another; style names in the app stay in English.

If the audio is a lecture rather than a conversation, structured study notes will hold up better than a summary.
