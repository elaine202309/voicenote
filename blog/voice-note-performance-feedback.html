How to Write Performance Feedback With a Voice Note

Written cold, performance feedback drifts into adjectives. Saying the year out loud first — one note per person, before any document exists — is what keeps the review specific enough to be fair and usable.

November 27, 2026 7 min read Elaine
Download on the App Store

Key takeaways

  • Feedback written from a blank page becomes adjectives; feedback spoken from memory becomes incidents.
  • One note per person, before you open the review form.
  • Replace labels with behaviour: “dismissive in reviews” is a verdict, “talked over two people in the March review” is evidence.
  • End with what you will do differently as their manager — feedback without that is a verdict.

Review season compresses a year into a form with three boxes. Faced with the boxes, most managers write the same six sentences they wrote last year, and the person on the other side learns nothing they did not already suspect.

The problem is not the form; it is that nothing was said out loud first. Speaking forces you back to incidents, and incidents are the only thing a review can be built on.

How to do it in VoiceNote

Step 1 — One note per person, before the form

Before you open the review document, open VoiceNote and record one note per direct report. The free plan takes up to two minutes per note, which is about the length of an honest walk through a year; Prime extends a single recording to fifteen minutes if the year was complicated.

  • Name three moments. Two where the work went well and one where it did not. Specific dates or projects — not impressions.
  • Say what changed. What this person can do now that they could not do in January. That sentence is usually the fairest thing in the whole review.
  • Say the sentence you would not put in writing yet. The blunt version — “I do not trust his estimates” — is not the final text, but it tells you what the review actually needs to address.
  • Say what you want next year. One or two behaviours, phrased as things they could do next month.

Step 2 — Apply Professional Email

Open the note, tap AI Style Conversion and choose Professional Email. The style turns the recording into a structured, respectful written form — greeting, body, close — which is close to the shape a review comment needs.

What it cannot do is know the person, the expectations of their level, or your company's rating scale — and it must not decide the rating for you. Use it for structure and tone, then edit the substance yourself.

Step 3 — Rewrite to evidence, behaviour, ask

Cut the draft to three parts and a closing line:

  • Evidence. The moment, the impact, and a number where one exists: “cut the release checklist from 40 items to 12, and the last two releases shipped without a rollback”.
  • Behaviour. What they did that produced it — and, for the hard part, what they did rather than what they are. “In the March review he answered over two colleagues before they finished” is usable; “he is difficult” is not.
  • Ask. One or two behaviours for next year, small enough to start in January and specific enough to recognise.
  • Your line. One sentence about what you will change as their manager — clearer priorities, earlier feedback, fewer meetings. Without it, the review reads as a judgement handed down.

Then read it aloud. If you would not say the sentence to the person's face, rewrite it until you would.

Why speaking first produces fairer reviews

Writing invites performance. Faced with a form, most managers write something defensible and slightly flattering, because the document may be read by HR or by the next manager.

Speaking to yourself has no audience to manage. You say the real version: what worried you, what surprised you, which month the person actually turned a corner. That is the material the written review needs.

It also catches recency. Reviews written in December describe November; reviews spoken from three named moments describe the year. The moment you name a project from March, the year stops collapsing into the last quarter.

A worked example

A made-up example. A team lead records a note about a senior engineer before touching the review form.

Spoken note, before the form

“Okay, Daniel. The good: the migration in April — he rebuilt the rollout plan in a week and we did not lose a single customer, and he did it without asking for help, which for him is new. Also he started reviewing other people's code, which he never used to do. The hard part: he is difficult in reviews. Specifically in March he talked over Priya and Tomas twice, and in June he dismissed the accessibility concerns in about four seconds and we had to redo them in August. And his estimates are optimistic — the migration was two weeks late because of it. What I want next year: he runs a review without overriding people, and his estimates come with a range instead of a date. What I should do differently: I gave him feedback in the corridor in June instead of in the moment, and I did not back Priya up.”

Written review, after the rewrite

What went well
You rebuilt the April migration plan in a week and we kept every customer live — the first time you have done that without escalating for help. You also started reviewing other people's code, which changed how the team works, not just what it ships.

Where it needs work
In the March design review you talked over Priya and Tomas twice, and in June the accessibility concerns were closed in under a minute, which cost us a redo in August. The pattern is speed in the moment, cost later. Your estimates are also optimistic: the migration ran two weeks past the date you gave, and the team absorbed that at the end of a quarter.

What I am asking for next year
Run one review per quarter without overriding anyone, and give estimates as a range with the risk named.

What I will do differently
I gave you the June feedback in a corridor in July instead of on the day, and I did not back Priya up in the room. Both are on me.

What changed: “difficult in reviews” became two dated incidents, the praise became something the team can verify, and the review ends with the manager owning his part.

Before you submit

  • Swap the names test. Read the review as if it were about a different report. If it would still fit, it is not specific enough.
  • No surprises. Anything in the review should already have been said in the last six months; if it has not, say it before the meeting, not in it.
  • Check every number. Dates, percentages, release counts — an error here discredits the whole document.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing in adjectives. “Proactive”, “strong communicator”, “needs to improve ownership” — none of them tell the person what to do on Monday.
  • The sandwich. Opening with praise to soften criticism teaches people to distrust praise.
  • Saving the real feedback for the meeting. A review should not be the first time someone hears it.
  • Letting the style pick the rating. The wording can be improved by a tool; the judgement, and the consequences, are yours.
  • Recording somewhere people can overhear. This is personal data about a colleague — record alone, and delete the audio when the review is written.

Frequently asked questions

Can VoiceNote write the whole review for me?

It can structure what you said. It cannot know the person, their level expectations or the rating, so the substance — evidence, behaviour, ask — has to come from you.

How long should the note be per person?

Two minutes is enough for a straightforward year; five to fifteen for someone who changed role or had a difficult year. One note per person keeps the reviews from blurring into each other.

Is it appropriate to record notes about a colleague?

Recording your own thinking is normal management practice. Treat the audio as sensitive: record alone, do not share it, and delete it once the written review exists.

How is this different from a professional email?

The email article is about writing to someone with a request. Here the output is a judgement about a person's work, which has a higher bar: evidence, fairness, and no surprises.

What about the conversation itself?

Do not script it. Rehearse the three sentences you must say — the evidence, the ask, and your own part — and leave the rest open. The written review is the record; the conversation is where it becomes useful.

Which languages does VoiceNote support?

VoiceNote transcribes 46 languages. Record in the language you will write the review in; style names in the app stay in English.

Before you write anyone else's review, it is worth doing your own year — managers who skip it tend to write the same feedback every year.
